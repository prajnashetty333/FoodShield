# FOODSHIELD Step 8B Diagnostic Report

## 1. Objective
1. Why is Step 8B replacement so high?
2. Which specific importer-commodity-year shocks remain difficult or impossible to replace?

## 2. Data and scope
- Period: 2010-2023, but 2010 is excluded from capacity statistics.
- Six commodities: Wheat, Rice, Maize, Palm Oil, Sugar, Sunflower Oil.
- Scope: Rank-1 shocks only. Capacity-valid scenarios only.

## 3. Why replacement is so high
- Mean replacement rate: 0.9983
- Median replacement rate: 1.0000
- Mean Tier 1 capacity coverage: 38635.49x
- Median Tier 1 capacity coverage: 59.71x
- Mean total capacity coverage: 2982673.87x
- Median total capacity coverage: 112.97x

## 4. Capacity explanation
Type A dominance is strongly associated with enormous Tier 1 HEEC relative to lost supply. In many cases, existing suppliers have historical trade expansions that far exceed the shocked lost supply.

## 5. Type C analysis
Type C scenarios occur when new origins (Tier 3) are required because existing and historical suppliers are insufficient. There are 936 such scenarios.

## 6. Type D analysis
- Count: 45
- Share: 0.41%
- Largest unreplaced supply case: 110 - Maize - 2011 (7054440.0 tonnes)
- Lowest replacement rate case: 138 - Rice - 2011 (0.1329)

## 7. HEEC diagnostics
The distribution of HEEC is highly skewed, reflecting large major exporters with massive historical swings, while many smaller candidates have zero HEEC.

## 8. Commodity comparison
Comparison among commodities reveals differing capacity distributions. See CSV outputs for details.

## 9. Temporal comparison
Replacement feasibility remains broadly consistent, though specific shocks vary by year.

## 10. Key findings
1. High replacement is driven by existing relationships possessing large HEEC.
2. Type A is almost perfectly collinear with Tier 1 sufficient HEEC.
3. New origins are rarely strictly required.
4. Type D cases are structurally rare and represent cases where global spare capacity proxy falls short.
5. Zero-HEEC candidates form a large share of potential relationships but contribute no replacement.

## 11. Methodological interpretation
The HEEC assumption provides generous replacement because historical peaks in outward trade often dwarf the specific bilateral loss of a single importer.

## 12. Limitations
1. HEEC is a historical trade-expansion proxy, not observed physical spare capacity.
2. No contract, inventory, shipping, tariff, or political data.
3. Domestic adaptation and commodity substitution are excluded.
4. Trade friction is represented only through tiers.
5. 2010 cannot be capacity-estimated.
6. Rank 1 is the primary shock analyzed here.
