# FOODSHIELD — Claim Strength Audit

| Original Claim | Problem | Revised Claim |
|---|---|---|
| "Countries can replace their food supply." | Overclaims real-world outcome | "Most modeled supplier-shock losses can be replaced under the historical trade-expansion capacity proxy." |
| "Food systems are resilient." | Vague, overgeneralizes | "Most modeled scenarios fall into the Type A existing-network replacement pathway." |
| "Food security is guaranteed." | Uses "guaranteed" and "food security" | "The model evaluates trade replacement feasibility, not food security itself." |
| "99.83% of food supply is secure." | Uses "secure" | "99.83% of lost supply is replaced on average under the historical export-expansion capacity proxy." |
| "87% of countries are resilient." | Equates scenarios to countries, uses "resilient" | "87.04% of scenarios can be resolved through current suppliers alone." |
| "Trade guarantees food security." | Causal policy claim, uses "guarantees" | "Trade replacement feasibility can inform monitoring of structural dependencies." |
| "The model proves resilience." | Uses "proves" and "resilience" | "The model demonstrates high theoretical replacement feasibility under historical proxies." |
| "Even with 75% capacity loss countries remain safe." | Uses "safe" and implies physical capacity | "The headline modeled replacement result is robust to substantial reductions in the historical capacity proxy." |
| "Will solve the dependency problem." | Uses "will solve" | "Could support policies focused on network diversification." |
| "Predicts future recovery." | Uses "predicts" and "future" | "Provides a basis for understanding counterfactual historical shocks." |
