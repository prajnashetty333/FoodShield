# FOODSHIELD — Figure Message Map

**Figure 01**
- **Question answered**: How concentrated are food imports prior to a shock?
- **One-sentence takeaway**: Pre-shock concentration identifies exposure to a dominant supplier, but it does not reveal whether alternative trade relationships can absorb the lost supply.
- **Does NOT prove**: Does not prove that concentration causes food insecurity or an inability to replace supply.
- **Presentation Location**: Slide 1 (The Problem)
- **Classification**: Core

**Figure 02**
- **Question answered**: How much supply is lost when the largest supplier disappears?
- **One-sentence takeaway**: The modeled Rank-1 shock is substantial, causing a mean loss of approximately 67.17% of baseline imports.
- **Does NOT prove**: Does not prove this shock will happen or represents a historical event.
- **Presentation Location**: Slide 3 (The Shock)
- **Classification**: Core

**Figure 03**
- **Question answered**: Where does the replacement supply come from?
- **One-sentence takeaway**: Replacement relies overwhelmingly on current suppliers, reflecting the strength of existing network pathways.
- **Does NOT prove**: Does not prove physical replacement is guaranteed or affordable.
- **Presentation Location**: Slide 4 (The Surprise)
- **Classification**: Core

**Figure 04**
- **Question answered**: What are the specific pathways for recovery?
- **One-sentence takeaway**: 87.04% of scenarios follow the Type A existing-network pathway, demonstrating high feasibility within current relationships.
- **Does NOT prove**: Does not prove that 87% of countries are resilient.
- **Presentation Location**: Slide 5 (The Pathways)
- **Classification**: Core

**Figure 05**
- **Question answered**: Which country-commodity systems face persistent constraints?
- **One-sentence takeaway**: There are 224 repeated Type C and 9 repeated Type D systems that require targeted policy focus.
- **Does NOT prove**: Does not prove these are the only vulnerable countries globally.
- **Presentation Location**: Slide 6 (The Exceptions)
- **Classification**: Supporting

**Figure 06**
- **Question answered**: How robust is the replacement rate to capacity reductions?
- **One-sentence takeaway**: The headline replacement rate is robust to substantial reductions in the historical capacity proxy.
- **Does NOT prove**: Does not prove the system is safe under a 75% capacity loss.
- **Presentation Location**: Slide 7 (The Stress Test)
- **Classification**: Core

**Figure 07**
- **Question answered**: How does capacity restriction alter replacement pathways?
- **One-sentence takeaway**: Restricting capacity shifts scenarios toward historically dependent and new-origin pathways, showing structural sensitivity.
- **Does NOT prove**: Does not prove these are observed historical transitions.
- **Presentation Location**: Slide 7 (The Stress Test, alternative to Figure 06)
- **Classification**: Supporting/Core (depending on slide count)

**Figure 08**
- **Question answered**: Is higher concentration associated with lower replacement?
- **One-sentence takeaway**: The scatter shows substantial dispersion in modeled replacement outcomes at higher supplier concentrations, but remains descriptive.
- **Does NOT prove**: Does not establish causality between concentration and lack of replacement.
- **Presentation Location**: Supplementary material
- **Classification**: Supplementary
