# FOODSHIELD — Decision Framework

This decision-tree style framework provides an actionable but non-prescriptive structure for evaluating resilience pathways. It is a policy interpretation of the existing FOODSHIELD model, not a new analytical model.

START
↓
Identify country–commodity system
↓
Observe largest supplier
↓
Apply modeled Rank-1 shock
↓
Assess current supplier replacement
↓
**If sufficient → TYPE A (Existing-network resilient)**
- *Immediate decision question:* Which current suppliers provide the modeled replacement volume?
- *Medium-term resilience question:* How can we maintain current diversification?
- *Long-term strategic question:* Are current suppliers structurally stable?
- *Data ideally examined outside FOODSHIELD:* Current trade contracts, shipping routes.
- *FOODSHIELD contribution:* Identifies that the modeled loss can be fully replaced by current-year supplier relationships under the historical export-expansion capacity proxy.
↓
**If insufficient → Assess historical suppliers**
↓
**If sufficient → TYPE B (Historically recoverable)**
- *Immediate decision question:* Which historically observed suppliers could potentially be re-engaged, subject to real-world feasibility?
- *Medium-term resilience question:* How can market access be preserved with past partners?
- *Long-term strategic question:* What real-world factors should be investigated to understand why historically observed suppliers are not current suppliers?
- *Data ideally examined outside FOODSHIELD:* Tariffs, historical trade agreements, changes in supplier domestic policy.
- *FOODSHIELD contribution:* Identifies scenarios where modeled full replacement relies on previously observed trade relationships.
↓
**If insufficient → Assess new-origin requirement**
↓
**If sufficient → TYPE C (New-origin dependent)**
- *Immediate decision question:* Which alternative origins are already available through modeled trade relationships?
- *Medium-term resilience question:* Which additional origins could be commercially qualified?
- *Long-term strategic question:* How can sourcing dependence be diversified across origins?
- *Data ideally examined outside FOODSHIELD:* Trade agreements, tariffs, quality requirements, commercial qualification, political/geopolitical conditions.
- *FOODSHIELD contribution:* Identifies scenarios where modeled full replacement requires a new origin.
↓
**If insufficient → TYPE D (Structurally constrained)**
- *Immediate decision question:* What is the share of residual unreplaced loss?
- *Medium-term resilience question:* What structural constraints prevent global supply from replacing the loss?
- *Long-term strategic question:* What strategic contingency options exist (e.g., domestic production, substitution)?
- *Data ideally examined outside FOODSHIELD:* Logistics capacity, port infrastructure, transportation costs, domestic reserves.
- *FOODSHIELD contribution:* Identifies scenarios where even the modeled new-origin pathway cannot completely replace the lost supply under the HEEC proxy.
