# FOODSHIELD — Policy Matrix

| Profile | Definition | Observed/model output | Decision interpretation | Potential policy direction | Monitoring question | What FOODSHIELD does NOT establish |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TYPE A** | Existing-network resilient | Current-year foreign suppliers other than the shocked supplier can fully replace the modeled lost supply under the HEEC capacity proxy. | The modeled loss can be fully replaced by current-year supplier relationships under the historical export-expansion capacity proxy. | Maintain current supplier diversification and monitor supplier concentration. | Is supplier concentration becoming more concentrated over time? | This does not establish that future suppliers will actually expand exports or provide equivalent replacement. |
| **TYPE B** | Historically recoverable | Current-year suppliers cannot fully replace the modeled loss, but historically observed suppliers can complete replacement. | The modeled loss requires reactivation of previously active trade relationships for full replacement. | Preserve historical supplier relationships and maintain market access. | Are historical supplier relationships degrading beyond revival? | This does not establish that historical suppliers have guaranteed physical spare capacity to return. |
| **TYPE C** | New-origin dependent | Current-year suppliers plus historically observed suppliers cannot fully replace the modeled loss, so Tier 3 new origins are required for full modeled replacement. | Full modeled replacement requires contribution from Tier 3 new origins under the specified model assumptions. | Develop additional origins and diversify sourcing geography. | Are there qualifying alternative exporters available globally? | This does not establish whether new origins are politically or contractually feasible. |
| **TYPE D** | Structurally constrained | Even after considering current suppliers, historically observed suppliers, and qualifying new origins under the HEEC proxy, the modeled loss cannot be fully replaced. | The modeled trade-replacement pathways remain insufficient; additional investigation is required to determine why. | Investigate structural constraints and examine external contingency planning. | What external constraints prevent full modeled replacement? | FOODSHIELD itself does not identify the specific real-world cause or causal driver of this constraint. |
